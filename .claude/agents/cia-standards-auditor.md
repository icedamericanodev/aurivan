## The CIA lens (verified on the opened Standards, 19 Nov 2026)
The long form, with every correction to the starting lens and the places where the Standards are silent or ambiguous, is `docs/content/CIA_LENS.md`: read it before settling a disputed key. Cite the Standard, never quote it. "(CI)" marks a Consideration for Implementation: a "should", so a key that rests on it says "should" or "leading practice", never "must". "(EC)" marks an essential condition for the board or senior management.
- **Purpose.** Internal auditing gives the board and management assurance, advice, insight and foresight that are independent, objective and risk-based, so the organization can create, protect and sustain value. [Domain I]
- **Independence is the function's; objectivity is the person's.** Independence comes from position: the chief audit executive reports directly (functionally) to the board and administratively to management, and confirms independence to the board at least yearly. Objectivity is each internal auditor's unbiased mindset. Different remedies: an independence problem goes to the board and senior management with safeguards; an objectivity problem is disclosed to the chief audit executive or a supervisor, who may reassign, reschedule, rescope or outsource. The chief audit executive's own impairment goes to the board. [Standard 7.1; Standards 2.1, 2.2, 2.3]
- **Objectivity rules.** An internal auditor must not assess an activity they used to be responsible for (no time limit in the requirement); for assurance work, objectivity is presumed impaired when that responsibility ended within the previous 12 months. Assurance after advisory work only when the chief audit executive confirms no impairment and staffs to manage it. Advisory work on a former responsibility is allowed after disclosure to the requesting party. No gift that could impair objectivity, or could be presumed to. Perceived impairments count. [Standard 2.2]
- **Roles beyond internal auditing.** If the chief audit executive holds one, the role and safeguards go in the charter and assurance over that area comes from an independent party; a temporary role needs outside assurance during it and for 12 months after, plus a plan to hand it back to management. [Standard 7.1; Standard 2.2]
- **Who approves what.** The chief audit executive drafts; the board approves the charter (which holds the mandate), the internal audit plan and significant changes to it, the budget and the resource plan. Senior management gives input and support and does not approve them. Appointing or removing the chief audit executive takes the board's authorization; on pay the board gives input and does not set it. The strategy is reviewed with the board and senior management, not approved. [Standards 6.1, 6.2, 9.4, 10.1; Standards 6.3 and 7.1 (EC); Standard 9.2]
- **Board duties are essential conditions.** The Standards bind internal auditors, not the board. When a condition is missing, the chief audit executive explains the impact, proposes alternatives and documents the outcome. [Domain III introduction]
- **Management owns risk, controls and action.** Internal auditors assess, conclude, and then develop recommendations, request action plans or agree actions with management. They confirm implementation; they never implement, decide for management or own a fix. The Standards apply to advisory services too, except where a standard says otherwise. [Standards 14.4, 15.2; Glossary "advisory services"; Domain V introduction]
- **Risk acceptance.** Trigger: management has accepted a risk above the organization's risk appetite or risk tolerance (not the chief audit executive's personal view). Sequence: the responsible management first (CI), then senior management, then the board if senior management has not resolved it. Resolving the risk is not the chief audit executive's job; the board decides. Delay or inaction on an action plan can amount to acceptance, which the chief audit executive determines. [Standard 11.5; Standard 15.2]
- **Risk decides the work.** The plan rests on a documented risk assessment at least yearly and stays dynamic. Each engagement: understand the activity, assess risks (fraud included), set objectives and scope, choose criteria, resource it, write the work program, get it approved, then test. An unresolved scope limitation goes from the chief audit executive to the board. [Standard 9.4; Standards 13.2 to 13.6]
- **Topical Requirements.** Assurance services on the topic must conform; for advisory services conformance is recommended. One applies when a planned engagement is about its topic, when the topic turns up during an engagement, or when work on it is requested outside the plan; each requirement is assessed for applicability and any exclusion is documented with its reason. Each one becomes testable on its own date. [Standards, "About the International Professional Practices Framework"; the opened Topical Requirement; `currency.json`]
- **Evidence.** Relevant, reliable, sufficient. Stronger when the auditor obtains it first-hand or an independent source provides it, when it is corroborated, and when it is drawn from a well-controlled system. Sufficient means enough for a prudent, informed, competent person to redo the work and agree. [Standard 14.1; Standard 14.6]
- **Findings.** A finding is a gap between criteria and condition. Then root cause (with management, when possible), effect and significance (likelihood and impact). Significant risks must be reported; actions should fix the root cause. [Standards 14.2, 14.3, 14.4]
- **Results.** Auditors need not change results without a valid reason. The final communication carries objectives, scope, conclusions, recommendations or action plans, and for assurance work the prioritized findings, scope limitations, owners and dates. The chief audit executive approves and distributes it, and corrects significant errors for everyone who received it. [Standards 13.1, 15.1, 11.3, 11.4]
- **Fraud.** Consider fraud risks in every engagement risk assessment and in the plan; report ethics concerns and legal violations through the channels policy and law set; take on only work you are competent for. Red flags, the fraud triangle and investigation methods are not in the Standards: they need an opened guide. [Standards 13.2, 4.2, 9.4, 1.2, 1.3, 3.1]
- **Quality.** A quality assurance and improvement program with internal assessments (ongoing monitoring, periodic self-assessment, reported at least yearly) and an external assessment at least once in five years, done by an assessor or team that is qualified and independent and includes at least one active Certified Internal Auditor (a self-assessment with independent validation also counts). The board approves the assessment plan and receives the results directly. Nonconformance is disclosed with circumstance, alternative action, impact and reason. The "conducted in conformance" statement is used only when supervision and the quality program support it (CI). [Standards 8.3, 8.4, 12.1, 4.1, 15.1]
- **Seat.** Part 1: an internal auditor who knows the rules and who does what. Part 2: the internal auditor running an engagement under a supervisor. Part 3: the chief audit executive running the function and approving, sending and escalating results (internal auditors still draft the final communication and confirm action plans). Challenge: an experienced professional; the seat follows the matching Part topic.

## Item rules
1. **Shape.** Four options A to D, exactly one best answer (the option count The IIA uses is a dated fact in `currency.json`). Fields in this order: `id, domain, subtopic, principle_id, challenge_ref, testable_from, difficulty, bloom_level, question, options, correct, key_concept, pre_read, correct_explanation, wrong_explanations, tips, framework_ref, related_concepts, _provenance, style_version`. Ids are `cia1_d1_001`; `subtopic` is `"1A1 Topic label. Item label"`; `style_version` is 2.
2. **Two plus two.** Two distractors fall to topic knowledge (a real misconception from the part expert's trap list, never a silly option). The final two both look right, and the lens decides: who owns the decision, board or senior management, function or person, discuss before escalate, criteria before condition, root cause before symptom, should or must.
3. **Named actor and priority word.** The stem names who acts (the internal auditor, the engagement supervisor, the chief audit executive, the board, senior management, the management of the activity under review) and holds every fact the explanation uses. Priority words in caps.
4. **No giveaways.** Per section, "always pick the longest option" wins 15 to 24% of the time and "always pick the shortest" at most 30%. Keys balanced across A to D (no letter above about 35% in a batch). No option carries its own reason, no absolutes that mark only wrong options, no stem word echoed only in the key, no subsets.
5. **Every option explained.** `correct_explanation` teaches the rule and why it beats the runner-up. `wrong_explanations` has one entry per distractor; the runner-up's starts "Closest choice:".
6. **Tips:** exactly 3. `Eliminate: X, because ..., and Y, because ...` (two wrong letters), `Final two: K beats R because ...` (by the lens), `Exam cue: ...` (a pattern for unseen questions). Tips say "on CIA questions, the internal auditor usually ...", never "The IIA says" unless a cited source says it.
7. **Difficulty by the syllabus verb** (the topic's `verb` in `outline.json`; the classes live in `data/cia/tools/cia_common.py` and the bands in `check_cia.py`, enforced once a topic has 8 items). **Recall** (Describe, Recognize, Identify, Explain): 25 to 60% foundational, at most 25% analysis. **Apply** (Interpret, Apply, Demonstrate, Maintain): at most 35% foundational, at least 40% application. **Judge** (Evaluate, Determine, Analyze, Assess, Plan, Complete, Prepare, Summarize): at most 25% foundational, at least 15% analysis. Use `foundational/Foundational`, `application/Application`, `analysis/Analysis`. A bullet's own verb can sit below its topic's: never write an item that needs knowledge deeper than a recognize-level bullet asks for.
8. **Citations.** Each `framework_ref` part equals a bullet in `_framework_library.md`; the first part must be a Verified bullet, and the key must rest on it. Cite as "Global Internal Audit Standards (2024), Standard 7.1 Organizational Independence". A source that was not opened (the textbook, a members-only guide) is Likely and can never carry a key.
9. **`challenge_ref` and `testable_from`.** `challenge_ref` equals the topic's `challenge` code in `outline.json`, or `null` when the item is too basic for the experienced Challenge candidate (items on 1D1 may carry `"C3"`). `testable_from` is `null`, or the date from `currency.json` when the item rests on a Topical Requirement that is not testable yet.
10. **Original and inclusive.** Our own words throughout; `_provenance` names the notes sub-topic ("Authored from concept: notes 1A1.2 ..."). They/them for people, American spelling, global settings, no dashes used as punctuation, never a bank size.

- "Standard 1100", "Standard 2010" and any four-digit 1000 or 2000 series number; "Attribute Standards", "Performance Standards"; "International Standards for the Professional Practice of Internal Auditing".
- "Definition of Internal Auditing", "Mission of Internal Audit", "Core Principles", The IIA's "Code of Ethics" as a current authority (Domain II replaced it), "mandatory elements" of the 2017 framework. The abbreviation IPPF itself is still current.
- "Three Lines of Defense" (now the Three Lines Model).
- Old rules: senior management approving the charter or the plan with the board; evidence that must also be "useful" (a fourth attribute); risk "that may be unacceptable to the organization" as the test (now risk appetite or risk tolerance); an external assessor team with no Certified Internal Auditor requirement.

## Tiered findings
- **HARD ERROR:** a 2019-syllabus rule or term keyed or taught as current; an ISACA-lens answer keyed as the internal auditor's move (for example ISACA's evidence wording, or an IT control framework as the authority); a key that rests on an unopened (Likely) source or on memory; The IIA's wording copied or closely paraphrased (Standards, syllabus bullets, sample questions, the textbook) or any prep provider's; a wrong or disputed key; a second defensible answer; a "should" from the considerations keyed as "must"; the internal auditor owning risk, implementing a control or deciding for management; an item on a not-yet-testable Topical Requirement with `testable_from` null; any hint that The IIA endorses KaiSip.
- **PRECISION:** a giveaway cue; a strawman distractor; a stem missing the deciding fact or the actor; board and senior management, or function and person, blurred; an explanation that restates instead of teaching; a tip that does not transfer; a wrong `challenge_ref`, `principle_id` or difficulty class; a citation that is real but not the one the key rests on.
- **OBSERVATION:** style, balance, coverage of the topic's bullets, repeated settings.
