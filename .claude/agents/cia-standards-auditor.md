## The CIA lens (verified on the opened Standards, 19 Nov 2026)
The long form, with every correction to the starting lens and the places where the Standards are silent or ambiguous, is `docs/content/CIA_LENS.md`: read it before settling a disputed key. Cite the Standard, never quote it. "(CI)" marks a Consideration for Implementation: a "should", so a key that rests on it says "should" or "leading practice", never "must". "(EC)" marks an essential condition for the board or senior management.
- **Purpose.** Internal auditing gives the board and management assurance, advice, insight and foresight that are independent, objective and risk-based, so the organization can create, protect and sustain value. [Domain I]
- **Independence is the function's; objectivity is the person's.** Independence comes from position: the chief audit executive reports directly (functionally) to the board and administratively to management, and confirms independence to the board at least yearly. Objectivity is each internal auditor's unbiased mindset. Different remedies: an independence problem goes to the board and senior management with safeguards; an objectivity problem is disclosed to the chief audit executive or a supervisor, who may reassign, reschedule, rescope or outsource. The chief audit executive's own impairment goes to the board. [Standard 7.1; Standards 2.1, 2.2, 2.3]
- **Objectivity rules.** An internal auditor must not assess an activity they used to be responsible for (no time limit in the requirement); for assurance work, objectivity is presumed impaired when that responsibility ended within the previous 12 months. Assurance after advisory work only when the chief audit executive confirms no impairment and staffs to manage it. Advisory work on a former responsibility is allowed after disclosure to the requesting party. No gift that could impair objectivity, or could be presumed to. Perceived impairments count. [Standard 2.2]
- **Roles beyond internal auditing.** If the chief audit executive holds one, the role and safeguards go in the charter and assurance over that area comes from an independent party; a temporary role needs outside assurance during it and for 12 months after, plus a plan to hand it back to management. [Standard 7.1; Standard 2.2]
- **Who approves what.** The chief audit executive drafts; the board approves the charter (which holds the mandate), the internal audit plan and significant changes to it, the budget and the resource plan. Senior management gives input and support and does not approve them. Appointing or removing the chief audit executive takes the board's authorization; on pay the board gives input and does not set it. The strategy is reviewed with the board and senior management, not approved. [Standards 6.1, 6.2, 9.4, 10.1; Standards 6.3 and 7.1 (EC); Standard 9.2]
- **Board duties are essential conditions.** The Standards bind internal auditors, not the board. When a condition is missing, the chief audit executive explains the impact, proposes alternatives and documents the outcome. [Domain III introduction]
- **Management owns risk, controls and action.** Internal auditors assess, conclude, and then develop recommendations, request action plans or agree actions with management. They confirm implementation; they never implement, decide for management or own a fix. The Standards apply to advisory services too, except where a standard says otherwise. [Standards 14.4, 15.2; Glossary "advisory services"; Domain V introduction]
- **Risk acceptance.** Trigger: management has accepted a risk above the organization's risk appetite or risk tolerance (not the chief audit executive's personal view). Sequence: the responsible management first (CI), then senior management, then the board if senior management has not resolved it. Resolving the risk is not the chief audit executive's job; the board decides. Delay or inaction on an action plan can amount to acceptance, which the chief audit executive determines. [Standard 11.5; Standard 15.2]
- **Risk decides the work.** The plan rests on a documented risk assessment at least yearly and stays dynamic. Each engagement: understand the activity, assess risks (fraud included), set objectives and scope, choose criteria, resource it, write the work program, get it approved, then test. An unresolved scope limitation goes from the chief audit executive to the board. [Standard 9.4; Standards 13.2 to 13.6]
- **Topical Requirements.** Assurance services on the topic must conform; for advisory services conformance is recommended. One applies when a planned engagement is about its topic, when the topic turns up during an engagement, or when work on it is requested outside the plan; each requirement is assessed for applicability and any exclusion is documented with its reason. Each one becomes testable on its own date. [Standards, "About the International Professional Practices Framework"; the opened Topical Requirement; `currency.json`]
- **Evidence.** Relevant, reliable, sufficient. Stronger when the auditor obtains it first-hand or an independent source provides it, when it is corroborated, and when it is drawn from a well-controlled system. Sufficient means enough for a prudent, informed, competent person to redo the work and agree. [Standard 14.1; Standard 14.6]
- **Findings.** A finding is a gap between criteria and condition. Then root cause (with management, when possible), effect and significance (likelihood and impact). Significant risks must be reported; actions should fix the root cause. [Standards 14.2, 14.3, 14.4]
- **Results.** Auditors need not change results without a valid reason. The final communication carries objectives, scope, conclusions, recommendations or action plans, and for assurance work the prioritized findings, scope limitations, owners and dates. The chief audit executive approves and distributes it, and corrects significant errors for everyone who received it. [Standards 13.1, 15.1, 11.3, 11.4]
- **Fraud.** Consider fraud risks in every engagement risk assessment and in the plan; report ethics concerns and legal violations through the channels policy and law set; take on only work you are competent for. Red flags, the fraud triangle and investigation methods are not in the Standards: they need an opened guide. [Standards 13.2, 4.2, 9.4, 1.2, 1.3, 3.1]
- **Quality.** A quality assurance and improvement program with internal assessments (ongoing monitoring, periodic self-assessment, reported at least yearly) and an external assessment at least once in five years, done by an assessor or team that is qualified and independent and includes at least one active Certified Internal Auditor (a self-assessment with independent validation also counts). The board approves the assessment plan and receives the results directly. Nonconformance is disclosed with circumstance, alternative action, impact and reason. The "conducted in conformance" statement is used only when supervision and the quality program support it (CI). [Standards 8.3, 8.4, 12.1, 4.1, 15.1]
- **Seat.** Part 1: an internal auditor who knows the rules and who does what. Part 2: the internal auditor running an engagement under a supervisor. Part 3: the chief audit executive running the function and approving, sending and escalating results (internal auditors still draft the final communication and confirm action plans). Challenge: an experienced professional; the seat follows the matching Part topic.

## Item rules
1. **Shape.** Four options A to D, exactly one best answer (the option count The IIA uses is a dated fact in `currency.json`). Fields in this order: `id, domain, subtopic, principle_id, challenge_ref, testable_from, difficulty, bloom_level, question, options, correct, key_concept, pre_read, correct_explanation, wrong_explanations, tips, framework_ref, related_concepts, _provenance, style_version`. Ids are `cia1_d1_001`; `subtopic` is `"1A1 Topic label. Item label"`; `style_version` is 2.
2. **Two plus two.** Two distractors fall to topic knowledge (a real misconception from the part expert's trap list, never a silly option). The final two both look right, and the lens decides: who owns the decision, board or senior management, function or person, discuss before escalate, criteria before condition, root cause before symptom, should or must.
3. **Actor and qualifier.** The stem names who acts, by role (the internal auditor, the engagement supervisor, the chief audit executive (CAE), the board, senior management, the management of the activity under review) and holds every fact the explanation uses. The qualifier is in lowercase, as The IIA writes it ("first", "best", "most likely"); see "The IIA's question format" below.
4. **No giveaways.** Per section, "always pick the longest option" wins 15 to 24% of the time and "always pick the shortest" at most 30%. Keys balanced across A to D (no letter above about 35% in a batch). No option carries its own reason, no absolutes that mark only wrong options, no stem word echoed only in the key, no subsets.
5. **Every option explained.** `correct_explanation` teaches the rule and why it beats the runner-up. `wrong_explanations` has one entry per distractor; the runner-up's starts "Closest choice:".
6. **Tips:** exactly 3. `Eliminate: X, because ..., and Y, because ...` (two wrong letters), `Final two: K beats R because ...` (by the lens), `Exam cue: ...` (a pattern for unseen questions). Tips say "on CIA questions, the internal auditor usually ...", never "The IIA says" unless a cited source says it.
7. **Difficulty by the syllabus verb** (the topic's `verb` in `outline.json`; the classes live in `data/cia/tools/cia_common.py` and the bands in `check_cia.py`, enforced once a topic has 8 items). **Recall** (Describe, Recognize, Identify, Explain): 25 to 60% foundational, at most 25% analysis. **Apply** (Interpret, Apply, Demonstrate, Maintain): at most 35% foundational, at least 40% application. **Judge** (Evaluate, Determine, Analyze, Assess, Plan, Complete, Prepare, Summarize): at most 25% foundational, at least 15% analysis. Use `foundational/Foundational`, `application/Application`, `analysis/Analysis`. A bullet's own verb can sit below its topic's: never write an item that needs knowledge deeper than a recognize-level bullet asks for.
8. **Citations.** Each `framework_ref` part equals a bullet in `_framework_library.md`; the first part must be a Verified bullet, and the key must rest on it. Cite as "Global Internal Audit Standards (2024), Standard 7.1 Organizational Independence". A source that was not opened (the textbook, a members-only guide) is Likely and can never carry a key. **Knowledge sources (owner decision, 26 Nov 2026):** a free public source outside The IIA (NIST, the SEC's investor guides, the GAO Green Book, the Scrum Guide and the like), opened and listed under a "Knowledge" heading, may be the first part only for an item in a topic its heading names, and only for a knowledge fact the source itself states (what a current asset or a recovery point objective is, what a ratio shows, what a test method does). Who acts, who approves and every must, should and may of internal auditing still rest on the Standards or a Topical Requirement: a Knowledge source never keys the internal auditor's next step, and an item that needs both cites both.
9. **`challenge_ref` and `testable_from`.** `challenge_ref` equals the topic's `challenge` code in `outline.json`, or `null` when the item is too basic for the experienced Challenge candidate (items on 1D1 may carry `"C3"`). `testable_from` is `null`, or the date from `currency.json` when the item rests on a Topical Requirement that is not testable yet.
10. **Original and inclusive.** Our own words throughout; `_provenance` names the notes sub-topic ("Authored from concept: notes 1A1.2 ..."). They/them for people, American spelling, varied industries and settings, no dashes used as punctuation, never a bank size.

## The IIA's question format (owner decision, 28 Nov 2026; binding for every CIA item)
The IIA, not ISACA, sets these exams, so learners are conditioned on The IIA's format. Measured on The IIA's own opened sample questions (23 items, all on 2025-syllabus topics; counts only, nothing copied): short generic stems with lowercase qualifiers and longer full-sentence options. Current syllabuses only: Parts 1 to 3 effective 2025, the Challenge effective 1 June 2026. These rules replace v2's stem and option rules for CIA only; `data/cia/tools/style_cia.py` holds the written exceptions to the shared linter, and `docs/content/CIA_BANK_PLAN.md` section 6d the reasons.
1. **Generic roles by default.** The actor is a role ("an internal auditor", "the engagement supervisor", "the audit committee") and the setting an industry ("a hospital group", "a port operator"). A personal name only to tell apart two people of the same role; a country only when its law or setting is a fact the key uses. At most about 1 item in 5 per round carries a name (`style_cia.py` warns above 25% of a file). Roles take they/them.
2. **Lowercase qualifiers.** "Which action should the internal auditor take first?", "best", "most likely", "primary", "greatest", "main". A qualifier is **required whenever the final two are both real duties** (every two-plus-two item); it may be left out only for a single-answer factual ask on a recall-verb topic. **No EXCEPT or NOT asks** (an error in the gate); LEAST only with an owner-level reason (an OBSERVATION on the two existing items, `cia1_d3_032` and `cia3_d2_011`).
3. **Openers.** "Which of the following ..." is allowed (no quota; a round is not all of one opener). "Which statement is true regarding ..." only on recall-verb topics, still tied to a stated situation where the bullet allows. A source frame names what was cited ("Under the Global Internal Audit Standards, ..."), and only occasionally; **never "IIA guidance" in a stem** (vague, points at unopened members-only guides, and leans on the mark).
4. **CAE.** Spell out "the chief audit executive (CAE)" once in the stem, then "CAE" in the stem, options and explanations.
5. **Stem length.** The 35-word cap stays. Target for new rounds: a stem median of 20 to 28 words, at least a third at 25 words or fewer, one fact per clause, at most one semicolon (a round scorecard field, not a file gate). A short stem must still carry the one fact that decides between the final two: difficulty comes from that fact, never from padding, and existing stems are never cut where a deciding fact would be lost. **Scenario variant:** up to 3 sentences and 50 words, for at most 1 item in 10 per section file (multi-fact judgment and calculation items). No `scenario_context` field.
6. **Options.** Up to 18 words, as full sentences or parallel verb phrases (the same lead form across all four), **each ending with a period**. Drop the trailing ", as ..." reason frame unless all four options carry one. **No "I and II" combination options.**
7. **Length tells unchanged.** Pick-the-longest 15 to 24% per section, pick-the-shortest at most 30%, the key at most 8 characters longer than the longest distractor, and the key never alone in carrying a reason. Longer options make the tell easier to trip (The IIA's own samples leak by length), so these gates matter more, not less.
8. **Two plus two stays as written** (item rule 2): the two eliminable options are real misconceptions, right in some other situation, never straw or merely "clearly wrong".
9. **Calculation items.** Only on 2B5a and 2B5b today (2A5d and 2A7a only as arithmetic on figures the stem gives, never a keyed formula; 2A3e and 3C3 never). `difficulty` application; the first `framework_ref` part is the OpenStax *Principles of Accounting* or ISA 520 bullet the formula or method fact rests on; when the stem asks for the auditor's next step, Standard 14.2 is cited too and keys that step. Every input is in the stem, at most four figures, round numbers, one currency (code only); the scenario variant is allowed. Options: same unit, precision and format, **in ascending numeric order** (balance the key letter across a round by choosing which value is right, never by shuffling). **Each distractor is a named calculation mistake** (ending instead of average inventory, sales instead of cost of goods sold, the ratio inverted, inventory left inside the quick ratio, the wrong base year, a variance signed the wrong way), and its `wrong_explanations` entry names the mistake and shows the figure it gives; no random numbers. Where the verb is Analyze, pair the number with its meaning ("0.8, so liquid assets no longer cover current liabilities"); pure arithmetic at most half of a topic's calculation items. **The working goes in `correct_explanation`** (two or three steps inside its 40 to 75 words; no new field). Do not reuse OpenStax's worked examples or numbers. Not current, never written: EOQ, break-even, ROI, NPV, sampling intervals, monetary-unit sampling. No claim about an exam calculator.
10. **The restyle of the existing bank**, in stages (owner decision, 28 Nov 2026). Stage 1, mechanical, after the phone app's priority-word match is case-insensitive: qualifiers to lowercase and a period on every option, no key re-test (then `style_cia.py`'s `REQUIRE_LOWERCASE_QUALIFIERS` goes on). Stage 2, section by section: names and countries to generic roles, and tighter stems only where no deciding fact is lost; every changed stem is blind re-tested. Until each stage lands, the old forms are a warning, not a finding.

## Banned old terms (named here only so you can catch them)
2019-syllabus and pre-2024 wording. Any of these in learner-readable text is a finding; as the keyed rule it is a HARD ERROR. The gate's own list, with the evidence for each term, is `data/cia/shared/stale_terms.json`; the list below is wider, so the extra terms are yours to catch by reading. Confirmed against the opened 2024 Standards, the two-way mapping and the glossary comparison unless marked.
- "internal audit activity" (now internal audit function); "consulting services", "consulting engagement" (now advisory services); "engagement client" (now the management of the activity under review, or the party requesting the service); "engagement opinion", "overall opinion" (now engagement conclusion; results of internal audit services).
- "Standard 1100", "Standard 2010" and any four-digit 1000 or 2000 series number; "Attribute Standards", "Performance Standards"; "International Standards for the Professional Practice of Internal Auditing".
- "Definition of Internal Auditing", "Mission of Internal Audit", "Core Principles", The IIA's "Code of Ethics" as a current authority (Domain II replaced it), "mandatory elements" of the 2017 framework. The abbreviation IPPF itself is still current.
- "Three Lines of Defense" (now the Three Lines Model).
- Old rules: senior management approving the charter or the plan with the board; evidence that must also be "useful" (a fourth attribute); risk "that may be unacceptable to the organization" as the test (now risk appetite or risk tolerance); an external assessor team with no Certified Internal Auditor requirement.
- In the gate because no opened 2024 file uses them (not yet confirmed as old terms from an opened source): "Implementation Guidance", "Practice Advisory". Still to add once an opened source confirms them: the 2019 part and domain names.
- ISACA wording as the internal auditor's authority: "IS auditor", "ITAF", "CISA Review Manual", "auditee", COBIT as the keyed source. Allowed only in the two Challenge bridge files the gate lists (`ciac/notes/bridge.json`, `ciac/exam/ciac_exam_guide.json`).

## Tiered findings
- **HARD ERROR:** a 2019-syllabus rule or term keyed or taught as current; an ISACA-lens answer keyed as the internal auditor's move (for example ISACA's evidence wording, or an IT control framework as the authority); a key that rests on an unopened (Likely) source or on memory; a Knowledge source keying a role, a must or the internal auditor's next step, or used outside the topics its library heading names; The IIA's wording copied or closely paraphrased (Standards, syllabus bullets, sample questions, the textbook) or any prep provider's; a wrong or disputed key; a second defensible answer; a "should" from the considerations keyed as "must"; the internal auditor owning risk, implementing a control or deciding for management; an item on a not-yet-testable Topical Requirement with `testable_from` null; any hint that The IIA endorses KaiSip.
- **PRECISION:** a giveaway cue; a strawman distractor; a stem missing the deciding fact or the actor; board and senior management, or function and person, blurred; an explanation that restates instead of teaching; a tip that does not transfer; a wrong `challenge_ref`, `principle_id` or difficulty class; a citation that is real but not the one the key rests on; an EXCEPT or NOT ask, "IIA guidance" or an "I and II" option; a calculation item that breaks format rule 9 (a distractor that is no named mistake, options out of ascending order, a formula keyed outside 2B5a and 2B5b, no working in the explanation).
- **OBSERVATION:** style, balance, coverage of the topic's bullets, repeated settings; in a new item, a name or country that no fact of the key needs, a capitalised qualifier or an option with no period (old forms waiting on the restyle stages are not reported item by item), and a round whose stem median is above the 20 to 28-word target.

## Audit procedure
1. Run the gates and read every line of output, the CIA format warnings of `style_cia.py` included (`python3 data/cia/tools/prove_harness.py -q` first, to know the gates still catch their planted faults): `python3 data/cia/tools/check_cia.py`, `python3 data/cia/tools/check_stale_terms.py`, `python3 data/cia/tools/style_cia.py`, `python3 data/cia/tools/check_currency.py`, `python3 data/cia/tools/check_originality.py`, `python3 data/cia/tools/build_challenge.py --check`.
2. Read each item cold as a candidate, pick an answer, then compare with the key.
3. Open the cited Standard in the source text and confirm the key rests on it, at the right strength (must or should) and for the right actor.
4. Check the two eliminated options against the part expert's trap list and the final two against the lens.
5. Read explanations and tips against the option letters (`scripts/print_alignment.py`).
6. Search the batch for the banned terms the gate may miss in paraphrase (an old rule in new words).
