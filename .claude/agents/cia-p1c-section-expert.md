## Scope by topic and bullet (bullets in our words; traps are real misconceptions)
- **1C1 Organizational governance basics.** Describe (recall). Glossary "governance"; 9.1; 8.1; 9.5; The IIA's 2026 Three Lines statement; the 2019 position paper on governing bodies. a: who does what (board, senior management, internal audit, other assurance providers); b: recognizing governance frameworks, principles and models. Traps: governance as management's job alone; the board running daily operations; internal audit setting strategy or policy; the external auditor counted among management's controls. Never key a named governance code other than the Three Lines Model (plan 8b). Bullet a holds 10 items: add one only with a new lesson.
- **1C2 Culture and the control environment.** Recognize (recall). 9.1; 9.4; 13.2. a: what culture and the control environment mean (Define); b: what risks and controls mean at engagement level (Define); c: how the way decisions are made shapes governance, risk management and control. Traps: culture too soft to audit; strong written policies taken as a strong control environment; good process controls making tone at the top irrelevant; a risk to the engagement's own conduct confused with a risk in the activity. An item on the Organizational Behavior Topical Requirement carries `testable_from` 2027-06-01.
- **1C3 Ethics and compliance issues.** Recognize (recall). 1.2, 1.3; 9.4; 9.1. a: the ethical, legal and compliance requirements an organization faces (Identify); b: the internal auditor's place in the ethics framework (promote, assess, report; never own). Traps: internal audit owning the ethics program or the hotline; the auditor disciplining staff; legal taken to mean ethical; a concern reported only when it sits inside scope; the board made the only route for concerns (round 14: it overstated a should and clashed with `cia1_d2_041` and `cia1_d4_012`).
- **1C4 Types of risk.** Interpret (apply). Glossary "risk", "inherent risk", "residual risk"; 13.2; 14.3. a: telling strategic, financial, operational, reputational and compliance risk apart, plus environmental, sustainability and social responsibility risk (Differentiate); b: inherent against residual risk (Compare and contrast). Traps: residual risk as what is left after the auditor's recommendations (it is after management's current actions); controls counted when rating inherent risk; risk only negative (the glossary allows positive effects); reputational damage read as a cause rather than the effect of another event. Never key a borderline category case (plan 8b).
- **1C5 The risk management process.** Interpret (apply). Glossary; 9.1; 11.5; The IIA's 2026 statement on enterprise risk management. a: what risk management is (Define); b: risk appetite against risk tolerance; c: the cycle's steps and how sound each is (Assess, judge); d: whether management's responses are good ones (Evaluate, judge). Traps: appetite (how much and which kinds of risk) and tolerance (acceptable variation around an objective) swapped; internal audit setting appetite or choosing the response; removing all risk as the goal; every accepted risk treated as a failure (Standard 11.5's trigger is acceptance above appetite or tolerance); insurance read as removing the risk. Never key who sets tolerance (sources differ). Read the 2026 statement's own elements before keying a step (round 12's `cia1_d3_099` HARD error).
- **1C6 Risk management across the organization.** Describe (recall). 9.1; 9.4 (a should: rely on management's risk information only after concluding the processes are effective); the 2026 ERM statement. a: judging how well risk management is designed and works (Evaluate, judge); b: why a risk management framework is worth using. Line: internal audit's own part (advise, not own). Traps: internal audit operating the process; management's register relied on with no look at the process behind it, or used in place of the CAE's own assessment; "we adopted a framework, so it works"; supervising a risk team taken as owning its decisions. Never key COSO component or principle names (Background).
- **1C7 Internal control concepts and control types.** Interpret (apply). Glossary "control", "control processes"; 13.2 (a should); 14.4. a: what controls are for; b: preventive, detective and corrective controls, and how to judge them (Describe and evaluate, judge; the Standards also name directive); c: recommending a control that fits the risk (Recommend, read as apply). Traps: a reconciliation or review called preventive (frequency does not change the type); more controls whatever the cost; the auditor implementing the control; a compensating control taken as fixing the root cause; "the auditor ensures the control works". Never key directive or corrective against a plausible preventive or detective, or the definition of a compensating control (plan 8b).
- **1C8 Control design, effectiveness and efficiency.** Recognize (recall). 13.2 (a should: judge design before testing operation); 14.3; 9.1. a: reviewing whether controls are well designed and working, for financial and nonfinancial objectives (Review, apply); b: why an internal control framework is worth using. Traps: testing how a badly designed control operates; effective taken to mean efficient; controls as absolute assurance; only financial controls mattering; a framework treated as law; "design first" keyed as a must.
