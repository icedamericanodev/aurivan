## Scope by topic and bullet (bullets in our words; traps are real misconceptions)
- **1A1 Purpose of Internal Auditing.** Describe (recall). Domain I; Glossary. a: what the function is for and what the organization gains; b: the three conditions that make it most effective (competent people conforming to the Standards, independent positioning with direct accountability to the board, auditors free from undue influence). Traps: purpose stated as managing risk, running controls or catching fraud; serving management only (it serves the board and management, and the public interest); positioning or conformance read as optional; the pre-2024 statement name. Bullet a: round 14 cut an item that repeated `cia1_d1_032`; a new one must teach what `cia1_d1_001`, `053`, `055` and `032` do not.
- **1A2 The mandate and who sets it** [Challenge A1]. Explain (recall). Principle 6; 6.1; 6.3 (EC); Domain III introduction. a: what the function may do, its role and duties; b: how the CAE supplies the information to set or refresh the mandate; c: what the board decides and what senior management contributes. Traps: the CAE setting or approving the mandate; senior management approving it; a mandate given by law treated as needing no charter; essential conditions read as rules the CAE can enforce (the CAE explains the impact, proposes alternatives, documents the outcome); the mandate settled once and never revisited.
- **1A3 Internal audit charter requirements.** Recognize (recall). 6.2; 6.1; 7.1. a: the parts a charter must hold (purpose, commitment to the Standards, mandate with scope and service types, the board's expectations of management's support, position and reporting lines, any role beyond internal auditing with its safeguards); b: why the board and senior management discuss it; c: why the board approves it. Traps: approval by senior management alone or jointly with the board (the 2017 rule); the CAE approving a draft they wrote; the annual plan or budget listed as charter content; charter (the document) and mandate (the authority) confused.
- **1A4 Assurance versus advisory services.** Interpret (apply). Glossary; Domain V introduction; 13.2 to 13.4; 14.2; 2.2. a: what counts as assurance; b: limited against reasonable assurance (Differentiate, apply); c: what counts as advisory; d: who sets an advisory job's scope (agreed with the requesting party); e: choosing the right service (Determine, judge: analysis items belong here). Traps: assurance scope set by the management of the activity; advisory work outside the Standards, or advice that takes management's decision; "reasonable" read as absolute; assurance after advisory work in the same area always barred (allowed when the CAE confirms no impairment and staffs to manage it). Bullet b: never key a definition or ranking beyond plan 8b's line.
- **1A5 Kinds of assurance engagements.** Describe (recall). Glossary "assurance services"; 6.1 (CI); the syllabus line (classification only, plan 8b). a risk and control assessments; b third parties and contract compliance; c IT security and privacy; d performance and quality; e operational, financial and regulatory compliance; f culture; g management reporting. Traps: operational (efficiency, effectiveness) against compliance (adherence to a rule); internal audit's financial work taken for the external auditor's opinion; a culture audit taken for a staff survey; what each audit consists of (no opened source: concept level only). An item on the Third-Party or Organizational Behavior Topical Requirement carries `testable_from`.
- **1A6 Kinds of advisory work.** Describe (recall). Glossary "advisory services"; Domain V introduction; 2.2; 7.1; the syllabus line. a risk and control training; b system design and development; c due diligence; d data privacy; e benchmarking; f helping management assess its own controls; g process mapping. Traps: the auditor designing, approving or signing off the system or control; a voting seat on the project; later assuring their own design with no safeguard; facilitating a self-assessment taken as owning its results; a significant risk met in advisory work dropped as "only advice" (note 1A6.2).
- **1A7 Threats to internal audit independence** [Challenge A2]. Identify (recall). Principle 7; 7.1; 6.3 (EC); 8.1; 8.2; 10.1; 9.4; 13.3. a: CAE reporting lines that do not fit (functional to the board, administrative to management); b: the board's part (authorizes appointment and removal, meets without management, gives input on pay); c: the CAE's part (confirms independence at least yearly; tells the board of an actual or perceived impairment); d: budget limits that restrict the work; e: scope limitations and cut access. Traps: independence (function) and objectivity (person) swapped; an impairment told to senior management only; an administrative line to the chief financial officer read as always fine or always a breach (safeguards; assurance over that officer's area is the issue); a scope limitation settled quietly with the manager; the board setting the CAE's pay; an engagement scope limitation confused with a cut to the charter's scope (note 1A7.4).
- **1A8 Internal audit and the Three Lines Model** [Challenge A3]. Recognize (recall). 7.1; 2.2; 9.5; The IIA's 2026 Statement of Position on the Three Lines Model (cited as "The IIA's 2026 statement", never as a Standards must; library rule 7). a: the model and who sits where; b: first- and second-line duties that would impair independence; c: safeguards when auditors do, or seem to do, that work. Traps: internal audit owning the risk register, setting appetite or choosing responses; second-line roles called independent like internal audit; a safeguard letting internal audit assure its own second-line work (an independent party must); a temporary role with no hand-back plan and no outside assurance during it and for at least 12 months after; the pre-2020 model name; wording from the superseded 2020 paper.
