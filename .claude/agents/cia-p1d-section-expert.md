## Scope by topic and bullet (bullets in our words; traps are real misconceptions)
- **1D1 Fraud triangle, fraud risks and schemes.** Describe (recall). Glossary "fraud"; the syllabus line (the three conditions by name). a: the triangle (motivation, opportunity, rationalization); b: spotting where fraud could happen (Recognize); c: common schemes (Identify). Traps: fraud and error confused (intent decides); controls read as reaching all three sides (they mostly reduce opportunity); management override forgotten; "a small amount is not fraud". Never key named schemes (lapping, kiting, skimming) or whether all three sides must be present (plan 8b). Tag "C3" only when an experienced Challenge candidate would still have to think; 3 of 6 carry it.
- **1D2 Weighing fraud risk in an engagement.** Determine (judge). 13.2 (consider fraud risks in every engagement risk assessment); 4.2; 9.4. a: considering fraud while planning (Recognize); b: judging which processes are most exposed (Assess: analysis items belong here). Traps: fraud considered only in a fraud audit or only when management asks; fraud risk assessed after fieldwork; strong past results read as low fraud risk; a formal, documented assessment demanded for every advisory job (it may be unnecessary). Beyond Standard 13.2, 1D2b needs a members-only guide.
- **1D3 Fraud risk management and red flags.** Evaluate (judge). 9.4; 4.2; 1.1 to 1.3; 13.1 (a should: issues needing immediate attention); the 2019 fraud position paper (Verified, should-level). a: judging how the organization manages fraud risk (Evaluate); b: finding and weighing red flags organization-wide and inside a process (Detect and assess); c: the auditor's part in reporting red flags found in the work (Recognize). Traps: confronting the suspect; concluding that fraud occurred (a legal conclusion) instead of reporting indicators; holding a red flag for the final communication; reporting to a manager who may be involved; one red flag taken as proof; a legal sign-off relied on as assurance when it is a control to test (`cia1_d4_042`). Never key a fixed red-flag list, or "never confront" and "bypass anyone involved" as CIA rules (sound practice, not Standards lines).
- **1D4 Controls that prevent and detect fraud.** Describe (recall). Glossary "control"; 9.1 (ethical culture); the syllabus line (authority levels may prevent fraud; hotlines, reconciliations and supervisory reviews detect it). a: tone at the top; b: segregation of duties; c: approval and authority limits; d: common detective controls. Traps: a hotline or reconciliation called preventive; authority limits called detective; segregation assumed to work under shared access or collusion; a written code taken as tone at the top; one detective control ranked above the others (the line does not rank them; round 3's `cia1_d4_026`); authority levels "classed as" preventive (the line says may; round 12).
- **1D5 Fraud investigation techniques and roles.** Recognize (recall). 3.1; 6.1 (a should); Glossary "advisory services" (forensic work as an example); 5.2; 9.5; the 2019 paper. a: what internal audit does and does not do in an investigation, set by its mandate and competence (Define); b: interviewing; c: investigating; d: testing for fraud; e: working with investigators and using their risk assessments, past cases, trends and whistleblower complaints. Traps: internal audit always leading the investigation; an auditor without the skills interviewing the suspect, or in an accusing style; the auditor calling the police on their own authority; investigators' findings ignored when planning. Never key interviewing technique, investigation steps or chain of custody (plan 8b); bullets b to d stay at recognize depth.
