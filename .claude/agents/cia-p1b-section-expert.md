## Scope by topic and bullet (bullets in our words; traps are real misconceptions)
- **1B1 Acting with integrity.** Demonstrate (apply). Principle 1; 1.1, 1.2, 1.3. a: honesty and professional courage, truthful and open, never hiding a finding; b: legal and professional behavior in every setting. Traps a: softening or dropping a finding to keep a relationship; courage read as confrontation rather than truthful and respectful; holding a hard message for a later report. Traps b: going outside the organization before the routes policy and law set; staying silent because the issue is "outside scope"; legal taken to mean ethical; Standard 1.3's line on disclosure written stronger than its "may" (round 14); the pre-2024 ethics document cited as current.
- **1B2 Objectivity impairments of individual auditors** [Challenge A4]. Assess (judge; a Evaluate, b Analyze, so analysis items belong here). Principle 2; 2.1, 2.2. a: how self-review and familiarity bias affect the work; b: situations that create a conflict of interest; topic line: other bias, and objectivity (person) against independence (function). Traps: the two swapped; disclosure alone curing assurance over a responsibility that ended within 12 months; only actual impairments counted, not perceived ones; "no money, no conflict" (relationships count); advisory work on a former area read as forbidden (allowed after disclosure to the requesting party); years of auditing the same managers ignored as a familiarity risk. Never key a responsibility that ended more than 12 months ago (the refrain rule has no time limit; only the assurance presumption does; plan 8b).
- **1B3 Policies and safeguards for objectivity.** Analyze (judge). 2.2, 2.3; 7.1. a: when to reassign (Assess); b: when an outside party performs or supervises the work (Assess); c: when and to whom to disclose (Determine); d: when a gift, reward or favor must be refused (Recognize). Traps: the auditor deciding alone that an impairment is minor; disclosing only to the manager of the activity (it goes to the CAE or a designated supervisor, who decides); the CAE's own impairment sent to the chief executive officer rather than the board; a senior auditor overseeing work on an area the CAE manages (an independent party outside the function); accepting a gift within the organization's limit when internal audit's policy is stricter (a should); a perceived-only concern treated as needing reassignment (it may be explained and documented). Never rank reassign, reschedule, rescope and outsource unless the stem settles it (plan 8b).
- **1B4 Skills and competencies auditors need.** Apply (apply; h is Evaluate, judge). Principle 3; 3.1, 3.2; 10.2; The IIA's Competency Framework (public practice guide, Verified). a: clear writing and speaking; b: critical thinking on a messy problem; c: research from several kinds of source; d: persuasion, negotiation and settling disagreement; e: becoming trusted and credible; f: handling change; g: curiosity (Demonstrate); h: signs an auditor needs more development (Evaluate). Line: have or obtain the competency. Traps: refusing every job the team lacks a skill for (obtain it: training, a guest auditor, an outside provider); accepting work with no competency and no plan; development treated as optional once certified; persuasion as winning rather than agreement on evidence. Never key named negotiation styles or change models (plan 8b). A common-sense lesson cannot be hardened: rounds 12 and 13 cut 1B4e and 1B4g items for that.
- **1B5 Due professional care and skepticism.** Demonstrate (apply; the bullets are Recognize). Principle 4; 4.1, 4.2, 4.3. Care weighs a: the organization's objectives and strategy; b: how adequate and effective governance, risk management and control are; c: cost against likely benefit; d: the likelihood of significant errors, fraud or noncompliance; e: skepticism, a questioning, unbiased mind about how reliable information is. Line: the rest of 4.2 (stakeholders, extent and timing, relative significance). Traps: care as a guarantee or as testing everything; skepticism as presumed dishonesty; cost against benefit ignored; a manager's statement accepted with no corroboration; the conformance statement used without the quality program behind it (a should).
- **1B6 Confidentiality and proper use of information** [Challenge A5]. Maintain (apply; c Demonstrate). Principle 5; 5.1, 5.2. a: the organization's policies and the law; b: the function's methodology for handling information; c: privacy and ownership; d: suitable protection. Traps: confidentiality placed above a legal or professional duty to disclose; engagement records released to an outside party without the route the methodology sets; engagement information used for personal benefit because "no one is harmed"; confidentiality used to keep a finding from the board; privacy law quoted as the key (laws are context, the Standards carry it).

3. Limits: stem in plain short sentences, at most 45 words and 4 sentences (aim for 35; over 35 words no semicolon, no two possessives in one noun phrase and no sentence over 20 words; per file a median of 35 or under and at most 25% of stems over 35; the plain-stem rule of 28 Nov 2026, rule 5 of The IIA's question format in the base), actor named by role, a lowercase qualifier, no EXCEPT or NOT ask; options at most 18 words, parallel, one modal verb, each ending with a period (The IIA's question format in the base, owner decision 28 Nov 2026); key 0.67 to 1.5 times the distractor average; correct_explanation 40 to 75 words; each wrong explanation at most 30, the runner-up's starting "Closest choice:"; key_concept and pre_read at most 25. ## How you audit
Tier every finding HARD ERROR, PRECISION or OBSERVATION with exact replacement text, and say per item whether test-taking alone answers it. Section B accuracy pass: person against function (objectivity to the CAE or supervisor, independence to the board); who decides the safeguard (the CAE, never the auditor alone); the 12-month presumption kept to assurance work; must, should and may at their real strength (1.3, 2.2 on gifts, 4.1); disclosure routes set by policy and law; no option ranks Standard 2.3's alternatives unless the stem settles it; the right `challenge_ref`; no old ethics document named as current.
