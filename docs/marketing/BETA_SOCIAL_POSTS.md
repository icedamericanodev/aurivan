**Angle:** an auditor's view of learning: a right answer a week later is stronger evidence than one straight after the explanation. This is why the plant works the way it does.
**Visual:** the 12–15 s Turning clip from Post 3, 1:1 crop, captions burned in. Or, as an image: `forms-reveal-grand-light.png`.
**CTA:** <BETA_FORM_URL> and kaisip.com.
**Length:** about 1,400 characters.

```
How do you know you've learned something, and not just seen it often?

In audit we'd call it evidence. A right answer straight after reading the explanation is weak evidence. A right answer a week later, with no hint, is much stronger.

That idea sits under the plant in KaiSip, my study app for the CISA® exam. The plant grows only from study that counts:
• To reach its second form: 10 or more questions answered in every domain, with no hint and not in a game
• Its third form: 15 questions right a week or more after you last saw them
• Its last form: 30 questions right a week later, a full timed mock, and steady work across every domain
Each form also asks you to know more of the exam outline.

When the plant gets there, the screen shows the learning first. Then the plant grows into a new form. We call that moment The Turning. The clip is real app footage at normal speed.

It isn't a score, and it doesn't predict your exam result. It's a quiet record that you did the slow, unglamorous part: coming back.

KaiSip is in a Google Play beta, free to use during the beta.
```

- **Target keyword:** "CISA exam" (descriptive), "IT audit", "IS auditor". Hashtags are few and descriptive: #CISA, #ITAudit, #InfoSec. No #ISACA.
