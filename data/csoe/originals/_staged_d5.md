## SEC releases
- SEC Release No. 33-8810 Section I (Introduction: reasonable assurance and reasonable detail)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/interp/2007/33-8810.pdf; checked Section I (pages 2-3): the "reasonable assurance" in the Commission's implementing rules relates to the FCPA's language; Exchange Act Section 13(b)(7) defines reasonable assurance and reasonable detail as the level of detail and degree of assurance that would satisfy prudent officials in the conduct of their own affairs; reasonableness is not an absolute standard of exactitude; it is an objective standard, but the terms do not imply a single conclusion or methodology.

- SEC Release No. 33-8810 Section II.A.1.a (Identifying financial reporting risks)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/interp/2007/33-8810.pdf; checked Section II.A.1.a (pages 13-14): management's evaluation of misstatement risk considers the entity's vulnerability to fraudulent activity (fraudulent financial reporting, misappropriation of assets and corruption); the extent of fraud-risk work is commensurate with the company's size and complexity; the risk of material misstatement due to fraud ordinarily exists in any organization, regardless of size or type; the absence of an identified fraud is not an indication that no fraud risk exists.
- SEC Release No. 33-8810 Section II.A.2.b (Implementing procedures to evaluate evidence of the operation of ICFR)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/interp/2007/33-8810.pdf; checked Section II.A.2.b (pages 27-29) and footnote 43 (page 28): footnote 43 quotes COSO's 1992 framework defining self-assessments as evaluations in which persons responsible for a particular unit or function determine the effectiveness of controls for their activities; self-assessments by personnel who operate the control generally provide less evidence because of lower objectivity; as ICFR risk rises management uses more objective people or direct testing, and where risk is high evidence ordinarily comes from direct testing or monitoring by more objective individuals; daily interaction may be sufficient in smaller companies where controls are centralized and few personnel are involved, but generally not with multiple management reporting layers or operating segments, where management ordinarily uses direct testing or ongoing monitoring-type procedures.
