## SEC releases
- SEC Release No. 33-8810 Section I (Introduction: reasonable assurance and reasonable detail)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/interp/2007/33-8810.pdf; checked Section I (pages 2-3): the "reasonable assurance" in the Commission's implementing rules relates to the FCPA's language; Exchange Act Section 13(b)(7) defines reasonable assurance and reasonable detail as the level of detail and degree of assurance that would satisfy prudent officials in the conduct of their own affairs; reasonableness is not an absolute standard of exactitude; it is an objective standard, but the terms do not imply a single conclusion or methodology.
- SEC Release No. 33-8810 Section II.A.2 (Self-assessments in ongoing monitoring)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/interp/2007/33-8810.pdf; checked Section II.A.2 (pages 27-28) and footnote 43: ongoing monitoring includes self-assessment procedures; footnote 43 quotes COSO's 1992 framework defining self-assessments as evaluations in which persons responsible for a particular unit or function determine the effectiveness of controls for their activities; evidence from self-assessments by personnel who operate the control generally provides less evidence because of their lower objectivity; where ICFR risk is high, management relies on direct testing or on monitoring by more objective people.

## COSO
- COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework (2013) Executive Summary (Effective internal control: operations objectives and external events)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_1df7d5dd38074006bce8fdf621a942cf.pdf; checked "Effective Internal Control" (page 8): when the system is effective, senior management and the board have reasonable assurance that the organization achieves effective and efficient operations when external events are unlikely to have a significant impact or can be reasonably predicted and mitigated, and understands the extent to which operations are managed effectively and efficiently when external events may have a significant impact; and the Limitations section (external events beyond the organization's control). Paraphrased only.
- COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework (2013) Executive Summary (Using the framework: roles of the board, senior management and auditors)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_1df7d5dd38074006bce8fdf621a942cf.pdf; checked "Using the Internal Control—Integrated Framework" (pages 9-10): senior management is accountable for internal control and to the board of directors; the board discusses the state of internal control with senior management, provides oversight, challenges management and seeks input from internal auditors, external auditors and others; internal auditors review their plans; independent auditors may audit or examine ICFR. Paraphrased only.
- COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework (2013) Executive Summary (Risk assessment: risk as an adverse effect on objectives)

Paraphrased only.
- COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework (2013) Executive Summary (Control activities: types and segregation of duties)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_1df7d5dd38074006bce8fdf621a942cf.pdf; checked "Components of Internal Control", Control Activities (page 4): control activities are actions established through policies and procedures, performed at all levels, at various stages within business processes and over the technology environment; they may be preventive or detective and manual or automated; segregation of duties is typically built in, and where it is not practical management selects and develops alternative control activities. Paraphrased only.

- COSO Thought Paper Risk Assessment in Practice (2012) The Risk Assessment Process (Assessing risk interactions)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_cdb2016e5bf64213a5ca726b7846856d.pdf; checked "The Risk Assessment Process" (page 2), written for the 2004 ERM framework: risks do not exist in isolation; even seemingly insignificant risks can interact with other events and conditions to cause great damage or create opportunity; the process includes assessing risk interactions (for example interaction matrices and bow-tie diagrams) before prioritizing; and page 3 on scales (ten-point scales imply unwarranted precision).
- COSO Thought Paper Risk Assessment in Practice (2012) The Risk Assessment Process (Common criteria, qualitative screening and quantitative analysis)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_cdb2016e5bf64213a5ca726b7846856d.pdf; checked "The Risk Assessment Process" (page 2): the first activity is developing a common set of assessment criteria across business units, corporate functions and capital projects; assessing risks may be done in two stages, an initial qualitative screening followed by more quantitative analysis of the most important risks.
- COSO Thought Paper Developing Key Risk Indicators to Strengthen Enterprise Risk Management (2010) Differentiating Key Performance Indicators from Key Risk Indicators (Early warning of emerging risks)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_6b2928dffbdf481dbf32971b3e9b9c85.pdf; checked the introduction (it builds on COSO's 2004 ERM framework and quotes its definition of ERM) and "Differentiating Key Performance Indicators from Key Risk Indicators" (page 1): KPIs mostly focus on results that have already occurred; KRIs are metrics that give an early signal of increasing risk exposures.
- COSO Thought Paper Embracing Enterprise Risk Management (2011) Keys to Success (Support from the top, incremental steps and a few top risks)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_b2d69c6e844642ae87ffa8fdbf80ced9.pdf; checked Section I, Keys to Success: Theme 1 (support from the board and senior management), Theme 2 (build ERM using incremental steps; experience does not support implementing all components in one effort), Theme 3 (focus initially on a small number of top risks; many organizations start by leveraging existing risk management resources and staff); and the Summary (small, incremental steps rather than implementing the complete ERM framework at once).
- COSO Enterprise Risk Management—Integrating with Strategy and Performance (2017) Executive Summary (Benefits of effective enterprise risk management)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_61ea5985b03c4293960642fdce408eaa.pdf; checked "Benefits of Effective Enterprise Risk Management" (pages 3-4): increasing the range of opportunities by considering both positive and negative aspects of risk; reducing performance variability, where performing ahead of schedule or beyond expectations may cause as much concern as falling short; improving resource deployment; enhancing resilience. - COSO Compliance Risk Management: Applying the COSO ERM Framework (2020) Introduction (Roles of management, the compliance function and the board)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_5f9c50e005034badb07f94e9712d9a56.pdf; checked "The scope and positioning of the compliance function in an organization" (page 5): managing and mitigating compliance risk is primarily the responsibility of management at all levels; the compliance function leads development of the compliance and ethics program, helps management understand the risks, evaluates execution and reports gaps; management executes the program and the board provides oversight.
- COSO Thought Paper Risk Appetite—Critical to Success (2020) Using Risk Appetite to Enhance Performance (Tolerance boundaries and management judgment)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_1607e24e43ad44bf96f725765cb8e78a.pdf; checked "Using Risk Appetite to Enhance Performance" and its Tolerance part (pages 19-21), written on the 2017 ERM framework: tolerance sets boundaries of acceptable variation in performance; outside the boundaries, management and the board challenge the organization, though business reasons may justify operating outside them; appetite alone cannot replace or supersede management judgment; management may revisit tolerance levels.
