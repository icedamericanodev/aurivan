## SEC releases
- SEC Release No. 33-8238 Section II.A.2 (Comments on the proposed definition of internal control)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/33-8238.htm; checked: a few commenters urged a broader definition covering enterprise risk management and corporate governance objectives; the SEC kept the focus on financial reporting because, "most important", Section 404 focuses on the element of internal control that relates to financial reporting; it also cited the reporting and cost burden and that independent accountants traditionally had not attested to controls outside financial reporting.

## COSO
- COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework (2013) Executive Summary (Components and principles)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_1df7d5dd38074006bce8fdf621a942cf.pdf; checked: the framework sets out seventeen principles representing the fundamental concepts of each component; an entity can achieve effective internal control by applying all principles; all principles apply to operations, reporting and compliance objectives. Principle names only; no COSO text reproduced in items.
- COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework (2013) Executive Summary footnote 1 (Board of directors as the governing body)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_1df7d5dd38074006bce8fdf621a942cf.pdf; checked footnote 1: the term "board of directors" encompasses the governing body, including board, board of trustees, general partners, owner or supervisory board. Same footnote in the ERM 2017 executive summary (https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_61ea5985b03c4293960642fdce408eaa.pdf, footnote 2).
- COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework FAQ (May 2013) Question 2 (Illustrative Tools and the ICEFR Compendium)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_8fdac5b8011e4cd08d566bf40b1ac2a8.pdf; checked: the Illustrative Tools templates focus on components and relevant principles, not the underlying controls, present only a summary of assessment results, are not an integral part of the framework, may not address all matters and can be modified by management; the ICEFR Compendium does not illustrate all aspects needed and is not sufficient to show each component and principle present and functioning; it supersedes the 2006 smaller public company guidance when the original framework is superseded.
- COSO Enterprise Risk Management—Integrating with Strategy and Performance (2017) Executive Summary (Foreword: what the update does)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_61ea5985b03c4293960642fdce408eaa.pdf; checked the foreword's list: the update enhances alignment between performance and ERM to improve the setting of performance targets, and recognizes the globalization of markets and operations and the need to apply a common, albeit tailored, approach across geographies.
- COSO Enterprise Risk Management—Integrating with Strategy and Performance (2017) Executive Summary (What enterprise risk management has achieved)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_61ea5985b03c4293960642fdce408eaa.pdf; checked: the updated framework positions risk in the context of an organization's performance, rather than as the subject of an isolated exercise.

## AICPA
- AICPA SASs Currently Effective Page (Statements on Auditing Standards apply to nonissuers)
  - Verified (opened 4 Nov 2026): https://www.aicpa-cima.com/resources/download/aicpa-statements-on-auditing-standards-currently-effective; checked: SASs apply to the preparation and issuance of audit reports for nonissuers (entities that are not issuers as defined by the Sarbanes-Oxley Act, and entities whose audits are not required to be conducted under PCAOB standards); the AU-C sections are current as of August 2026.
- AICPA Employee Benefit Plan SOC 1 Resource Center (Use of SOC 1 reports by plan auditors)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.aicpa-cima.com/resources/toolkit/ebp-soc-1-reports-and-service-organizations; checked: most employee benefit plans use service organizations (such as bank trustees); SOC reports, formerly known as SAS 70 reports, are often obtained and used by the auditor to understand the controls at the service organization and potentially reduce the amount of substantive testing; AU-C section 402 guides the independent auditor of an entity that uses a service organization.
