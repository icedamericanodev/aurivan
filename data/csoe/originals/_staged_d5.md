## SEC releases
- SEC Release No. 33-8238 Section II.A.2 (Comments on the proposed definition of internal control)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/33-8238.htm; checked: a few commenters urged a broader definition covering enterprise risk management and corporate governance objectives; the SEC kept the focus on financial reporting because, "most important", Section 404 focuses on the element of internal control that relates to financial reporting; it also cited the reporting and cost burden and that independent accountants traditionally had not attested to controls outside financial reporting.
- SEC Release No. 33-8238 Section II.A.3 (The 1992 COSO Report's operations objectives)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/33-8238.htm; checked: the release quotes the COSO Report that operations objectives "pertain to effectiveness and efficiency of the entity's operations, including performance and profitability goals and safeguarding resources against loss", and notes that safeguarding can also fall within other categories.

## COSO
- COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework (2013) Executive Summary (Components and principles)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_1df7d5dd38074006bce8fdf621a942cf.pdf; checked: the framework sets out seventeen principles representing the fundamental concepts of each component; an entity can achieve effective internal control by applying all principles; all principles apply to operations, reporting and compliance objectives. Principle names only; no COSO text reproduced in items.
- COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework (2013) Executive Summary footnote 1 (Board of directors as the governing body)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_1df7d5dd38074006bce8fdf621a942cf.pdf; checked footnote 1: the term "board of directors" encompasses the governing body, including board, board of trustees, general partners, owner or supervisory board. Same footnote in the ERM 2017 executive summary (https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_61ea5985b03c4293960642fdce408eaa.pdf, footnote 2).
- COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework (2013) Executive Summary (Introduction: related publications and precedence)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_1df7d5dd38074006bce8fdf621a942cf.pdf; checked the page before the Executive Summary: the Internal Control over External Financial Reporting compendium illustrates how the components and principles can be applied in preparing external financial statements; the 2009 monitoring guidance has similar applicability to the updated framework; neither the compendium, the monitoring guidance nor any other past or future guidance takes precedence over the framework.
- COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework (2013) Executive Summary (Front matter: COSO's sponsoring organizations)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_1df7d5dd38074006bce8fdf621a942cf.pdf; checked: COSO is a private sector initiative, jointly sponsored and funded by the AAA, AICPA, FEI, IMA and IIA; PwC is named as author.
- COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework FAQ (May 2013) Question 2 (Illustrative Tools and the ICEFR Compendium)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_8fdac5b8011e4cd08d566bf40b1ac2a8.pdf; checked: the Illustrative Tools templates focus on components and relevant principles, not the underlying controls, present only a summary of assessment results, are not an integral part of the framework, may not address all matters and can be modified by management; the ICEFR Compendium does not illustrate all aspects needed and is not sufficient to show each component and principle present and functioning; it supersedes the 2006 smaller public company guidance when the original framework is superseded.
- COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework FAQ (May 2013) Question 7 (Efficiency in designing and conducting internal control)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_8fdac5b8011e4cd08d566bf40b1ac2a8.pdf; checked the examples: specifying suitable objectives; focusing on risks above acceptance levels; coordinating risk identification and assessment across multiple objectives; controls that effect multiple principles reduce discrete, layered-on controls; one common language across operations, reporting and compliance.
- COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework FAQ (May 2013) Question 9 (Who was involved in the project)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_8fdac5b8011e4cd08d566bf40b1ac2a8.pdf; checked: PwC served as author and project leader under the direction of the COSO Board; an Advisory Council of practitioners and others served as a sounding board.
- COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework FAQ (May 2013) Question 10 (Regulators as observers)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_8fdac5b8011e4cd08d566bf40b1ac2a8.pdf; checked: the FDIC, GAO, IFAC, PCAOB and SEC participated as observers in all Advisory Council meetings and provided input throughout the project.
- COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework FAQ (May 2013) Question 15 (The 2009 Guidance on Monitoring Internal Control Systems)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_8fdac5b8011e4cd08d566bf40b1ac2a8.pdf; checked: the framework incorporates the guidance in COSO's 2009 Guidance on Monitoring Internal Control Systems, which will continue to be a reference document for management.

## AICPA
- AICPA SASs Currently Effective Page (Statements on Auditing Standards apply to nonissuers)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.aicpa-cima.com/resources/download/aicpa-statements-on-auditing-standards-currently-effective; checked: SASs apply to the preparation and issuance of audit reports for nonissuers (entities that are not issuers as defined by the Sarbanes-Oxley Act, and entities whose audits are not required to be conducted under PCAOB standards); the AU-C sections are current as of August 2026.
- AICPA Employee Benefit Plan SOC 1 Resource Center (Use of SOC 1 reports by plan auditors)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.aicpa-cima.com/resources/toolkit/ebp-soc-1-reports-and-service-organizations; checked: most employee benefit plans use service organizations (such as bank trustees); SOC reports, formerly known as SAS 70 reports, are often obtained and used by the auditor to understand the controls at the service organization and potentially reduce the amount of substantive testing; AU-C section 402 guides the independent auditor of an entity that uses a service organization.
