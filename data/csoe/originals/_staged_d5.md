## SEC releases
- SEC Release No. 33-8238 Section II.A.3 footnote 50 (The board and audit committee in the control environment)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/33-8238.htm; checked footnote 50 (attached to "board of directors" in the final ICFR definition, Section II.A.3): the COSO Report treats the composition of the board and audit committee, and how directors fulfill their financial reporting responsibilities, as key aspects of the control environment, including overseeing the reporting process and assessing management's accounting judgments and estimates (COSO Report at 130). Paraphrased only.
- SEC Release No. 33-8238 Section II.B.3.a footnote 68 (Home-country frameworks and the effectiveness statement)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/33-8238.htm; checked footnote 68: some home-country evaluation frameworks used for foreign companies do not require a statement on whether internal control is effective; management relying on such a framework must still state affirmatively whether its ICFR is, or is not, effective. Also checked footnote 67 (CICA Guidance on Assessing Control and the Turnbull Report as examples of other suitable frameworks) and the four suitability criteria in the same section (free from bias; reasonably consistent qualitative and quantitative measurements; sufficiently complete; relevant to an evaluation of ICFR).

## SEC staff guidance
- SEC Staff Statement on Management's Report on ICFR (May 16, 2005) Section F (Information technology issues)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.sec.gov/info/accountants/stafficreporting.htm; checked Section F: a separate, specific IT framework (footnote 20 names CobiT) is not required, though parts of available frameworks may be useful; no fixed list of general IT controls; management applies reasonable judgment about how IT systems affect ICFR; the staff does not believe it appropriate to exclude new IT systems and upgrades implemented late in the fiscal year from the scope of management's assessment, rejecting the analogy to acquired businesses in FAQ Question 3; not all testing must occur at year-end. The Commission kept the no-exclusion position in Release No. 33-8810, page 57 (https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/interp/2007/33-8810.pdf, opened 31 Oct 2026). Cite Section F only: the statement's material weakness wording (Section B, "more than a remote likelihood") predates Release No. 33-8810 and AS 2201.

## COSO
- COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework (2013) Executive Summary (Relationship of objectives and components)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_1df7d5dd38074006bce8fdf621a942cf.pdf; checked "Relationship of Objectives and Components": objectives are what an entity strives to achieve, components what is required to achieve them, and the organizational structure (operating units, legal entities and other) the third dimension of the cube; the three objective categories are the columns and the five components the rows. Paraphrased only.
- COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework (2013) Executive Summary (Introduction: a dynamic, integrated process for entities of any size)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_1df7d5dd38074006bce8fdf621a942cf.pdf; checked the Executive Summary's opening pages: internal control is not a serial process but a dynamic and integrated one; the framework applies to large, mid-size and small, for-profit and not-for-profit entities and government bodies; a smaller entity's system may be less formal and less structured yet still have effective internal control. Paraphrased only.
- COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework FAQ (May 2013) Question 5 (Effect on SOX 404 filers)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_8fdac5b8011e4cd08d566bf40b1ac2a8.pdf; checked Question 5: management's assertion on ICFR, such as SOX 404(a) and 404(b), is not expected to change; to transition, senior management assesses how the system of internal control applies the 17 principles, and the board oversees that assessment.
- COSO Thought Paper COSO in the Cyber Age (2015) Control Environment and Monitoring Activities (Foundational components for managing cyber risk)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_9af90248e5a34ae3addd148d256107c0.pdf; checked page 4 and the section "Control Environment and Monitoring Activities — Managing Cyber Risk is not possible Without Governance" (page 16): both components are foundational and must be present and functioning, or the organization is unlikely to understand cyber risks, deploy effectively designed control activities and respond appropriately. Paraphrased only.
- COSO Thought Paper Embracing Enterprise Risk Management (2011) Overview (Definition of ERM from the 2004 framework)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_b2d69c6e844642ae87ffa8fdbf80ced9.pdf; checked the sidebar in "Overview and the Question of 'Where to Start?'": COSO's 2004 framework defines ERM as a process effected by the board, management and other personnel, applied in strategy setting and across the enterprise, designed to identify potential events and manage risk within risk appetite, giving reasonable assurance on entity objectives. Paraphrased only.
- COSO Thought Paper Risk Assessment in Practice (2012) Inherent and Residual Risk (Definitions from the 2004 ERM framework)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_cdb2016e5bf64213a5ca726b7846856d.pdf; checked "Inherent and Residual Risk": in the 2004 ERM framework COSO defines inherent risk as the risk to an entity in the absence of any actions management might take to alter its likelihood or impact, and residual risk as the risk remaining after management's response to the risk.
- COSO Enterprise Risk Management—Integrating with Strategy and Performance (2017) Executive Summary (Management's and the board's guides to ERM)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_61ea5985b03c4293960642fdce408eaa.pdf; checked "Management's Guide to Enterprise Risk Management" (management holds overall responsibility for managing risk to the entity) and "The Board's Guide to Enterprise Risk Management" (oversight may include reviewing, challenging and concurring with management on proposed strategy and risk appetite, alignment with mission, vision and core values, significant business decisions and responses to performance fluctuations; approving management incentives and remuneration). Paraphrased only.
- COSO Enterprise Risk Management—Integrating with Strategy and Performance (2017) Executive Summary (Clearing up misconceptions: ERM for organizations of any size)
  - Verified (opened 31 Oct 2026): https://www.coso.org/_files/ugd/3059fc_61ea5985b03c4293960642fdce408eaa.pdf; checked the "Clearing up a few misconceptions" box: ERM can be used by organizations of any size; if an organization has a mission, a strategy and objectives and must make decisions that fully consider risk, ERM can be applied, from small businesses and community-based social enterprises to government agencies and Fortune 500 companies; its principles apply at all levels and across all functions. Paraphrased only.
